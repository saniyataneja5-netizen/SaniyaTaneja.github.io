KAVACH: a question paper no single person can open

Exam papers leak at single points of custody, where one person with access can photograph the paper days before the exam. KAVACH keeps the paper encrypted and splits its key across five independent custodians. The paper opens only when three of them agree, at the scheduled time, from the registered exam centre. Every refused attempt is recorded against the person who made it.

I built a working prototype with five role-based interfaces (exam authority, custodian, centre superintendent, auditor and candidate) and a public transparency portal. A simulation engine checks all four release conditions live, so you can watch an early unlock or a wrong-location request get refused and logged.

  • Threshold Three of five key shares submitted
  • Time Scheduled release window is open
  • Location Request comes from the registered centre
  • Identity Every custodian signature is valid

The prototype simulates the cryptography and the ledger. The full design uses AES-256-GCM encryption, Shamir's Secret Sharing for the 3-of-5 key split, Ed25519 signatures and a Hyperledger Fabric permissioned chain.
